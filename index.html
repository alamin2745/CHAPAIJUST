Our home, our identity

A district full of stories

Chapai Nawabganj lies in the north-western corner of Bangladesh, close to the Indian border. The Mahananda and Padma river systems have shaped its landscape, agriculture and everyday life. The district is widely associated with mango cultivation, the historic Gaur region and the distinctive Gambhira folk tradition.

History & architecture

Echoes of the past

The wider Gaur region preserves traces of Bengal's medieval history. Mosques, archaeological remains and historic sites connect today's communities with centuries of cultural exchange.

Choto Sona Mosque

One of the best-known monuments of the Gaur region, Choto Sona Mosque is admired for its stonework, carefully proportioned structure and ornamental details. It offers a glimpse into the architectural skill and artistic traditions of medieval Bengal.

Tahkhana Complex

Located in the historic Gaur area, the Tahkhana complex is associated with the region's Mughal-period heritage. Its surviving structures and setting make it an important stop for anyone interested in local history and architecture.

The Gaur Heritage

The historic Gaur landscape contains scattered archaeological remains and monuments that tell stories of political power, trade, religion and craftsmanship in earlier periods of Bengal's history.

The mango capital

A taste known across Bangladesh

Chapai Nawabganj is famous for its mango orchards, seasonal markets and varieties loved throughout Bangladesh. Kansat is one of the country's best-known mango trading hubs. During mango season, orchards and markets become central to local livelihoods, transport and commerce.

Living traditions

Gambhira: a voice of the people

Gambhira is a distinctive folk performance tradition strongly associated with Chapai Nawabganj. Through rhythmic songs, humour and conversations—often featuring Nana and Nati—it reflects everyday concerns, social observations and community wisdom. It is entertainment, but also a way to speak about society.

Nana and Nati

The familiar characters Nana and Nati use dialogue, wit and song to discuss social issues in a way that is accessible to audiences of different ages.

A heritage to preserve

Performers and cultural groups keep Gambhira alive through practice, festivals and public performances. Supporting local artists helps pass this tradition to future generations.

River and rural life

The riverside landscape is part of the district's everyday identity—shaping farming, local journeys, seasonal rhythms and memories shared by generations.

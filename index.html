Get to know our homeland

A land shaped by rivers, history and the mango season.

At the western edge of Bangladesh's Rajshahi Division, Chapai Nawabganj carries a distinctive landscape and a rich local identity. The district is known for fertile agricultural land, mango cultivation, historic settlements connected with medieval Gaur, and the rivers that have shaped life across the region. Its five upazilas are Chapai Nawabganj Sadar, Shibganj, Gomostapur, Nachole and Bholahat.

From the busy mango markets of Kansat to the calm banks of the Mahananda, from centuries-old monuments to the lively performance tradition of Gambhira, our district brings together nature, enterprise and culture.

Echoes of the past

Walk through our living history.

The district preserves remarkable traces of Bengal's Sultanate and Mughal periods. These places are more than monuments — they are chapters in the story of this land.

Shibganj • Sultanate heritage

Choto Sona Mosque

One of the region's best-known historic monuments, associated with the late fifteenth and early sixteenth centuries. Its carefully crafted architecture reflects the artistic legacy of the Bengal Sultanate.

Firozpur • Mughal-era site

Baroduari / Tahkhana Complex

The Tahkhana complex is associated with Prince Shah Shuja and the historic landscape around the Sona Mosque area. It adds another layer to the district's architectural heritage.

The flavour of our identity

Chapai Nawabganj — the land of mangoes.

When summer arrives, orchards turn green and golden, village roads fill with the fragrance of ripe fruit, and the famous Kansat market comes alive with growers, traders and buyers. Mango cultivation is not simply an agricultural activity here: it supports livelihoods, connects communities and carries the name of Chapai Nawabganj across Bangladesh.

Among the varieties associated with the district are Khirsapat (Himsagar), Langra, Fazli, Gopalbhog, Amrapali and Ashwina. The season, harvest calendar and availability vary by variety and year.

The voice of our people

Gambhira: culture with a voice.

Gambhira is a distinctive folk performance tradition centred around Chapai Nawabganj and the wider north-western region. Through rhythmic songs, humour, dialogue and the familiar characters of Nana and Nati, performers reflect on everyday life and social issues. It is entertainment, storytelling and community commentary woven together — a living expression of our cultural identity.

“A stage where humour speaks, stories live, and a community finds its voice.”
